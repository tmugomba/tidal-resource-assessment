Background

Why the Bay of Fundy has the world's biggest tides

The Bay of Fundy, between Nova Scotia and New Brunswick, pushes roughly 160 billion tonnes of water in and out with every tide cycle — more than the combined flow of all the freshwater rivers and streams on Earth. At the head of the bay, in the Minas Basin, the vertical tidal range exceeds 16 metres between low and high water: the largest tidal range recorded anywhere in the world.

Methodology, data sources & known limitations

Everything on this page traces back to a real, citable source or a documented modelling choice. This tab lays out exactly how, so the numbers above can be checked rather than taken on trust.

Methodology, step by step

  1. Harmonic height prediction. Water level is modelled as the sum of three tidal constituents — M2 (principal lunar semi-diurnal, 12.42h), S2 (principal solar semi-diurnal, 12.00h), and N2 (larger lunar elliptic semi-diurnal, 12.66h) — the standard technique used in real tide-table prediction.
  2. Constituent fitting. Amplitude and phase for each constituent were fitted by least-squares regression against 30 days of real predicted water levels for Burntcoat Head. A first attempt using only 1 day of data failed — those three periods are too close together (under 30 minutes apart) to separate from a single day — which is itself documented as a finding, not hidden.
  3. Velocity approximation. Current velocity is not measured directly from height. It's approximated as the rate of change of the fitted height curve, rescaled so its peak matches a user-adjustable reference velocity. This is a simplification — see Known Limitations below.
  4. Power density. Kinetic power per square metre of swept area uses the standard fluid power flux equation, P/A = 0.5 × seawater density × velocity³ — the same cubic relationship used for wind power.
  5. Turbine output. Converts power density into an actual kW figure for a specific real turbine (Orbital O2/O2-X class: 600 m² swept area, 2 MW rated at 2.5 m/s), including the realistic pitch-control cap that holds output flat above rated speed rather than letting it grow unbounded.
  6. Validation. The fitted height model is checked directly against the real 30-day dataset it was derived from, with a live RMSE readout in the Calculator tab. Real measured current velocity (from a FORCE seabed ADCP) is shown as a standalone reference rather than falsely overlaid, since the height model isn't yet anchored to real calendar time — flagged explicitly rather than glossed over.

Known limitations

Velocity is derived, not measured

Current velocity comes from the rate of change of the fitted height curve, rescaled to a reference peak — not a direct harmonic fit to real current measurements. True current harmonic analysis would need a long ADCP time series (weeks to months), which wasn't available for this iteration.

Height model isn't anchored to real calendar time

The M2/S2/N2 fit uses "hours since an arbitrary data-pull window" rather than absolute epoch time, so it can't yet predict for a specific real date. This is why the real ADCP data (April 2018) is shown as a standalone reference rather than overlaid against the model.

Turbine swept area is a reasonable substitute, not a confirmed O2-X figure

The O2-X's exact rotor dimensions aren't separately published. The turbine output model reuses the predecessor O2's verified 600 m² swept area, which shares the same twin-rotor architecture.
